Anterior cingulate cortex: PM versus PBS

Analysis date

20 October 2026

Study question

This secondary analysis compares anterior cingulate cortex expression between the PM nasal-exposure and PBS control groups of GSE297423. Only samples without alpha-synuclein preformed-fibril injection enter this comparison. The animals belong to the original humanized alpha-synuclein model; results should be interpreted within that experimental context. Other arms in the deposited study are preserved in the source collection but are outside this contrast. (Zhang et al. 2025)

Materials and methods

Input and sample selection

The analysis starts from the depositor’s gene-count table, rather than realigning FASTQ files. Ensembl identifiers are the analysis keys; the gene symbols, biotypes, and chromosomes supplied in that table remain annotations. Counts are required to be finite, nonnegative integers, and duplicate gene identifiers cause an explicit failure rather than an implicit aggregation.

The eight selected libraries are Control5, Control6, Control7, Control8, PMA2775, PMA2776, PMA2778, and PMA2779. Their GEO sample-to-library mapping determines group allocation. All selected biological samples are retained. No unverified batch, sex, or pairing variable is added to the model.

Differential expression and effect sizes

Genes must have at least 10 counts in at least four of the eight selected samples. PyDESeq2 fits a negative-binomial generalized linear model with design ~condition, median-of-ratios size factors, and a two-sided Wald contrast for PM relative to PBS. Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p values target an FDR of 0.05 within this contrast, with independent filtering and Cook’s filtering enabled. (Love et al. 2014; Muzellec et al. 2023; Benjamini and Hochberg 1995)

The unshrunken maximum-likelihood coefficients, standard errors, Wald statistics, p values, and adjusted p values are saved before effect-size shrinkage. The additional shrunken coefficient and uncertainty columns use the apeGLM-style heavy-tailed shrinkage implemented by PyDESeq2. Shrinkage stabilizes effect-size displays; it does not replace the preserved Wald test results. (Zhu et al. 2019)

At four biological replicates per group, the default requirement of at least seven replicates for Cook’s count replacement is not met. Gene-level Cook’s filtering can therefore leave p values unavailable, even though no biological sample is removed. Missing adjusted p values caused by independent filtering are distinguished from these unavailable nominal tests.

Quality assessment and displays

Sample summaries include total assigned gene counts, detected genes, size factors, and mitochondrial and ribosomal RNA count fractions. These fractions summarize the supplied count matrix and do not replace read-level quality assessment. PCA uses a blind variance-stabilizing transformation and the 500 most variable retained genes, centered without variance scaling. Heatmap rows are standardized only for visualization. Neither PCA nor the top-ranked heatmap is an independent validation dataset.

This heterogeneity is reported rather than used to remove samples.

Differential expression

There are 1,167 genes with FDR < 0.05: 658 higher and 509 lower in PM. Of these, 45 additionally have an absolute shrunken log2 fold change of at least 1. That additional effect-size summary is a descriptive selection, not a separate test of a minimum effect size.

Nominal Wald p values are available for 19,225 genes and adjusted p values for 17,360 genes. The 1,875 unavailable adjusted p values include 10 Cook-filtered nominal tests and 1,865 genes with a nominal test that were not retained for multiple-testing adjustment by independent filtering. These categories should not all be called outlier genes.

Interpretation and limits

The comparison supports a tissue-level expression difference in this specific PM exposure model. It does not identify which cell populations changed expression, distinguish cell-state shifts from cellular composition, or establish a human disease mechanism. Sample heterogeneity and unmeasured covariates remain relevant despite high sample correlations. The analysis also does not recreate read alignment, transcript quantification, or the other experimental arms of the original study.

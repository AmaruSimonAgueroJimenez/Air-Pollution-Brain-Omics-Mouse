Hippocampal ozone: age and APOE genotype

Analysis date

19 October 2026

Study question and analysis status

E-MTAB-14917 contains hippocampal RNA-seq from male human APOE3- or APOE4-targeted-replacement mice, with filtered-air and ozone groups in two starting-age strata. The ozone regimen used 0.8 ppm for seven hours per day on five days, followed by nine days of filtered air, repeated for eight cycles. The source age categories refer to 3 or 17 months at exposure onset, rather than establishing age at tissue collection. This analysis keeps age, genotype, and exposure distinct and estimates ozone-versus-filtered-air contrasts within each age-by-genotype stratum. (Nakamya et al. 2025)

Gene-level inference is complete for 24 libraries. The results below are read from the completed contrast tables. Read-level QC, quantification QC, and figures are displayed when their corresponding completed outputs are available.

Materials and methods

Design and biological replication

The complete design contains 24 sequenced animal samples, with three libraries in each of eight combinations of starting age, APOE genotype, and exposure. A library represents a biological sample; reads and atlas cells are not additional exposure replicates. No animal is excluded from the configured analysis. The deposited metadata contain one strain entry that differs from the other entries; it is preserved and documented rather than silently corrected.

Read-level QC and adapter decisions

The initial fastp screen examines the first 200,000 read pairs per library. This is a bounded, position-dependent QC sample, not a random subsample or an all-read quality assessment. Adapter evidence from paired-read overlap determines whether at least 0.5% of sampled reads require adapter trimming. For a flagged library, fastp then processes the complete paired FASTQ files to produce derived adapter-trimmed input for quantification. Quality filtering, length filtering, poly-G trimming, and duplicate removal are disabled in this workflow. Original downloaded FASTQ files are preserved.

Full-read fastp reports, where trimming is performed, remain available separately from the initial sampled-QC reports. Q30 percentages in the initial screen must therefore be interpreted as sampled-read summaries. Adapter-related pair removals are counted separately in the full-run records, including short adapter-dimer pairs; disabled quality and length filters do not imply that every original pair survives adapter processing. Quantification integrity is assessed from the complete library’s Salmon outputs and reference-to-transcript mapping, rather than inferred from that screen alone.

Transcript quantification and gene-level import

The workflow uses Salmon selective alignment against GENCODE M25 mouse transcripts with the whole GRCm38 primary genome as decoys. Library orientation is detected automatically, and sequence- and GC-bias models are enabled. Quantification uses all input read pairs or their complete derived adapter-trimmed equivalents. (Patro et al. 2017)

Tximport summarizes estimated transcript fragments to genes with countsFromAbundance="no", preserving sample-specific abundance-weighted effective gene lengths. DESeqDataSetFromTximport includes the corresponding gene-length offsets in the count model. Estimated fragments and TPM are exported separately; TPM values are not supplied as raw counts to DESeq2. Genes require at least ten rounded estimated fragments in at least three of the 24 libraries, using a rule independent of the group labels. (Soneson et al. 2015; Love et al. 2014)

The mouse reference does not provide allele-specific quantification of the human APOE3 and APOE4 replacement sequences. Genotype groups come from the experimental metadata. No APOE-allele expression estimate or verified allele-specific mapping performance is claimed. Counts aligned at the mouse Apoe/humanized APOE replacement locus are not interpreted as validated allele- or locus-specific expression measurements.

Four stratified contrasts and the global ozone test

The full negative-binomial model is ~age * genotype * exposure. Dispersions are estimated using all 24 libraries. Equivalent group-factor reparameterizations make each planned within-stratum ozone-versus-filtered-air effect a single coefficient for Wald testing and apeglm shrinkage. Original Wald maximum-likelihood effects and p values are preserved; shrunken effects are additional estimates for interpretation and visualization. (Love et al. 2014; Zhu et al. 2019)

Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment is reported within each of the four prespecified contrasts. A supplementary column, padj_across_four_strata, adjusts the combined p-value family across all four contrasts. These columns answer different multiplicity questions and are shown separately. Their discovery sets need not be nested: a different ranked p-value family can increase or decrease the adjusted value for an individual test. This p-value adjustment does not pool animals into a single exposure group. A global likelihood-ratio test compares the full model with ~age * genotype, using four degrees of freedom to ask whether adding exposure-related coefficients improves the fit. Its gene-level BH adjustment forms a separate family. The global test has no single signed ozone effect and is not used as a signed preranked-enrichment statistic. (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995)

Cook’s filtering remains enabled. The fixed count prefilter is followed by BH adjustment of the valid tests; adaptive independent filtering is disabled. Numerically nonconverged coefficient fits and genes with zero counts in all six animals of a stratum are flagged, with their exposure-test p values withheld before BH adjustment. Global full- and reduced-model convergence is checked separately. No unverified library batch or RIN covariate is invented. Evidence that ozone responses differ between strata requires an appropriate interaction comparison; differing numbers of significant genes alone are insufficient.

Relationship to the original publication

This is an independent raw-read reanalysis. The original publication used HISAT2/featureCounts and selected exposure-associated genes with nominal p < 0.05 and absolute log₂FC > 0.1. Here, Salmon/tximport quantification, the factorial count model, and BH FDR < 0.05 define a different analysis. Discovery counts must therefore not be described as reproducing the publication’s nominal-p gene lists. (Nakamya et al. 2025)

Interpretation and limits

The stratified comparisons estimate hippocampal tissue-level differences in the original mouse experiment. Sample numbers remain three per exposure group within each stratum even though the full model borrows information across the complete design. Genotype and starting-age labels do not make these libraries interchangeable with the PM cortex or hypothalamus studies. Mouse-reference quantification, the unprovided batch/RIN covariates, and the deposited strain inconsistency remain limitations. No spatially resolved ozone-response measurement or cell-type-specific exposure effect is generated by this pipeline.

Unequal discovery counts across the strata do not by themselves establish that APOE4 or older age increases susceptibility. The global four-degree-of-freedom test assesses whether any exposure-related term improves the model; it does not isolate an age-by-exposure or genotype-by-exposure interaction. Such a claim would require a corresponding interaction contrast and its own uncertainty and multiplicity assessment.

GO analyses of completed signed Wald contrasts, when available, are presented separately on the enrichment page. Their addition does not convert the global LRT into a directional pathway analysis or justify pooling contrasts from different brain regions and exposures.

### Read-level QC and adapter decisions

The initial fastp screen examines the **first 200,000 read pairs per library**. This is a bounded, position-dependent QC sample, not a random subsample or an all-read quality assessment. Adapter evidence from paired-read overlap determines whether at least 0.5% of sampled reads require adapter trimming. For a flagged library, fastp then processes the complete paired FASTQ files to produce derived adapter-trimmed input for quantification. Quality filtering, length filtering, poly-G trimming, and duplicate removal are disabled in this workflow. Original downloaded FASTQ files are preserved.

Full-read fastp reports, where trimming is performed, remain available separately from the initial sampled-QC reports. Q30 percentages in the initial screen must therefore be interpreted as sampled-read summaries. Adapter-related pair removals are counted separately in the full-run records, including short adapter-dimer pairs; disabled quality and length filters do not imply that every original pair survives adapter processing. Quantification integrity is assessed from the complete library's Salmon outputs and reference-to-transcript mapping, rather than inferred from that screen alone.

### Transcript quantification and gene-level import

The workflow uses Salmon selective alignment against **GENCODE M25 mouse transcripts** with the **whole GRCm38 primary genome as decoys**. Library orientation is detected automatically, and sequence- and GC-bias models are enabled. Quantification uses all input read pairs or their complete derived adapter-trimmed equivalents. [@Patro2017_Salmon]

Tximport summarizes estimated transcript fragments to genes with `countsFromAbundance="no"`, preserving sample-specific abundance-weighted effective gene lengths. `DESeqDataSetFromTximport` includes the corresponding gene-length offsets in the count model. Estimated fragments and TPM are exported separately; TPM values are not supplied as raw counts to DESeq2. Genes require at least ten rounded estimated fragments in at least three of the 24 libraries, using a rule independent of the group labels. [@Soneson2015_tximport; @Love2014_DESeq2]

The mouse reference does **not** provide allele-specific quantification of the human APOE3 and APOE4 replacement sequences. Genotype groups come from the experimental metadata. No APOE-allele expression estimate or verified allele-specific mapping performance is claimed. Counts aligned at the mouse `Apoe`/humanized `APOE` replacement locus are not interpreted as validated allele- or locus-specific expression measurements.

### Four stratified contrasts and the global ozone test

The full negative-binomial model is `~age * genotype * exposure`. Dispersions are estimated using all 24 libraries. Equivalent group-factor reparameterizations make each planned within-stratum ozone-versus-filtered-air effect a single coefficient for Wald testing and apeglm shrinkage. Original Wald maximum-likelihood effects and p values are preserved; shrunken effects are additional estimates for interpretation and visualization. [@Love2014_DESeq2; @Zhu2019_apeglm]

Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment is reported **within each of the four prespecified contrasts**. A supplementary column, `padj_across_four_strata`, adjusts the combined p-value family across all four contrasts. These columns answer different multiplicity questions and are shown separately. Their discovery sets need not be nested: a different ranked p-value family can increase or decrease the adjusted value for an individual test. This p-value adjustment does not pool animals into a single exposure group. A global likelihood-ratio test compares the full model with `~age * genotype`, using **four degrees of freedom** to ask whether adding exposure-related coefficients improves the fit. Its gene-level BH adjustment forms a separate family. The global test has no single signed ozone effect and is not used as a signed preranked-enrichment statistic. [@Benjamini1995_FDR]

Cook's filtering remains enabled. The fixed count prefilter is followed by BH adjustment of the valid tests; adaptive independent filtering is disabled. Numerically nonconverged coefficient fits and genes with zero counts in all six animals of a stratum are flagged, with their exposure-test p values withheld before BH adjustment. Global full- and reduced-model convergence is checked separately. No unverified library batch or RIN covariate is invented. Evidence that ozone responses differ between strata requires an appropriate interaction comparison; differing numbers of significant genes alone are insufficient.

### Relationship to the original publication

This is an independent raw-read reanalysis. The original publication used HISAT2/featureCounts and selected exposure-associated genes with nominal p < 0.05 and absolute log₂FC > 0.1. Here, Salmon/tximport quantification, the factorial count model, and BH FDR < 0.05 define a different analysis. Discovery counts must therefore not be described as reproducing the publication's nominal-p gene lists. [@Nakamya2025_40141051]

## Interpretation and limits

The stratified comparisons estimate hippocampal tissue-level differences in the original mouse experiment. Sample numbers remain three per exposure group within each stratum even though the full model borrows information across the complete design. Genotype and starting-age labels do not make these libraries interchangeable with the PM cortex or hypothalamus studies. Mouse-reference quantification, the unprovided batch/RIN covariates, and the deposited strain inconsistency remain limitations. No spatially resolved ozone-response measurement or cell-type-specific exposure effect is generated by this pipeline.

Unequal discovery counts across the strata do not by themselves establish that APOE4 or older age increases susceptibility. The global four-degree-of-freedom test assesses whether any exposure-related term improves the model; it does **not** isolate an age-by-exposure or genotype-by-exposure interaction. Such a claim would require a corresponding interaction contrast and its own uncertainty and multiplicity assessment.

GO analyses of completed signed Wald contrasts, when available, are presented separately on the [enrichment page](enrichment.qmd). Their addition does not convert the global LRT into a directional pathway analysis or justify pooling contrasts from different brain regions and exposures.

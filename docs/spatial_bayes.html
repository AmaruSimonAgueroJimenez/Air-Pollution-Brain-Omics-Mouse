Bayesian detection and spatial baseline validation

An anatomy-aware reference model evaluated on held-out physical sections

Analysis date

20 October 2026

Introduction

Two Bayesian analyses address different targets for twenty fixed region–gene pairs. A hierarchical beta-binomial model estimates gene-detection probability, allowing physical sections to differ. A spatial Gaussian model estimates section-adjusted expected baseline log-expression; its limitations for sparse RNA counts are evaluated explicitly. Both quantify conditional uncertainty and use reserved whole sections. Actual measurements, model estimates and experimental pollution contrasts remain separate.

The spatial-expression baseline is a project-specific Bayesian basis regression, not an implementation of Tangram, BayesSpace or cell2location. The model uses proper priors and an analytically available posterior. Fixed-basis Bayesian regression provides the methodological background; the exact priors, nuisance terms and transformed-count assumptions used here are recorded with the implementation. (Rasmussen and Williams 2006)

Bayesian detection: methods

For gene gg, anatomy aa and physical section ss, YsagY_{sag} is the number of positive raw imaging counts among nsan_{sa} measured cells, including all zeros in the denominator. We use

Ysag∣psag∼Binomial⁡(nsa,psag),psag∣μag,κag∼Beta⁡(μagκag,(1−μag)κag). Y_{sag}\mid p_{sag}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n_{sa},p_{sag}),\qquad p_{sag}\mid\mu_{ag},\kappa_{ag}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\mu_{ag}\kappa_{ag},(1-\mu_{ag})\kappa_{ag}).

The beta-binomial marginal likelihood allows section-to-section heterogeneity, rather than treating all cells as independent replicates sharing one fixed probability. Proper independent priors are μ∼Beta⁡(0.5,0.5)\mu\sim\operatorname{Beta}(0.5,0.5) and ln⁡κ∼𝒩(ln⁡20,1.52)\ln\kappa\sim\mathcal N(\ln20,1.5^2). These choices are fixed; they do not use held-out expression. Integration uses a deterministic 64 × 48 prior-quantile grid, with grid sensitivity reported separately. Hypermean intervals use weighted discrete-grid quantiles. Known-section probability and held-out positive-count intervals use 8,192 fixed-seed mixture draws; full-reference new-section probability intervals use deterministic inversion of the weighted Beta-mixture CDF. This is numerical grid/Monte Carlo integration, not an exact continuous posterior or an MCMC convergence claim. (SciPy developers, n.d.) Section propensities are exchangeable within each anatomy and gene; this model does not separate biological AP trends from technical section variation.

Increasing the integration grid from 64 × 48 to 96 × 64 changes full-reference posterior mean probabilities by at most 0.00020 in ACA and 0.00054 in HY. New-section probability interval endpoints change by at most 0.0026 and 0.0036, respectively; discrete hypermean interval endpoints can move by about 0.020 and 0.018. Held-out count coverage changes from 97.89% to 98.05% in ACA and stays at 99.35% in HY. These checks describe finite numerical precision; high coverage with wide intervals does not establish predictive usefulness.

The known-section posterior probability uses that reference section’s observed numerator and denominator. It is a fitted estimate, not held-out prediction. Its credible interval differs from uncertainty in the anatomical hypermean μ\mu, and from the distinct distribution of a new section’s probability. New-section positive-count predictive intervals condition on the actual held-out denominator nn. These are probabilities or counts of detection, not expression magnitudes or spatially inferred molecules.

The same five whole-section folds and neighboring guard sections used below provide out-of-sample evaluation. A regional nonanatomical model is trained on the same permitted sections for comparison. Brier loss is a cell-weighted descriptive prediction score; positive-count interval coverage is reported over section–anatomy groups, with denominators and prior-only groups explicit. A held-out anatomy absent from training retains the specified prior and is not silently replaced with a learned anatomical pattern.

Spatial-expression baseline: methods

Data, target and anatomical information

All registered anterior-cingulate and hypothalamic cells from the unexposed male ABCA-2 donor are retained. Ten previously selected measured genes per region are modeled. Counts and coordinates remain unchanged. The response is natural-log transformed imaging count, yig=ln⁡(1+cig)y_{ig}=\ln(1+c_{ig}), including measured zeros. It is not log₂ fold change, RNA-seq normalized expression, or a protein measurement. (Zhang et al. 2023)

Anatomical substructures, standardized CCF coordinates and a finite anisotropic radial-basis expansion describe the spatial mean. The model can represent jumps between anatomical categories. Original section intercepts absorb slice-wide shifts. Expression-derived cell-class and subclass labels are excluded from the primary predictors, avoiding target information carried through atlas RNA-based annotations. Physical-section identities define the validation partitions.

Proper priors and posterior distributions

For each gene, the working model is

yig=ϕ(𝒓i,ai)⊤𝜷g+δs(i),g+ϵig,ϵig∼𝒩(0,σg2). y_{ig}=\phi(\mathbf r_i,a_i)^\top\boldsymbol\beta_g+\delta_{s(i),g}+\epsilon_{ig}, \qquad \epsilon_{ig}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_g^2).

Here 𝒓i\mathbf r_i denotes registered position, aia_i anatomical category and s(i)s(i) physical section. Coefficients and section effects have proper zero-centered Normal priors conditional on residual variance, with a proper inverse-Gamma prior for that variance. The Normal–Inverse-Gamma update yields Student-t posterior distributions after integrating coefficient and variance uncertainty. Anisotropy, prior strengths, nuisance variance and basis size are fixed for the primary analysis; sensitivity fits vary prespecified alternatives.

The map displays the spatial mean with the section offset averaged to zero. Two uncertainty quantities have different meanings:

  • The 95% latent-mean credible interval describes uncertainty about that section-adjusted mean under the model.
  • The 95% new-cell/new-section predictive interval also includes residual variation and an unseen section offset. This wider interval is evaluated against held-out measured cells.

The Gaussian likelihood is a working approximation for sparse transformed counts. It can assign probability below zero, outside the physical support of ln⁡(1+c)\ln(1+c). Negative fitted means, negative bounds and predictive mass below zero are retained and diagnosed. Estimates are not exponentiated into claims of expected molecule counts. Interval uncertainty is conditional on the chosen anatomy, basis, priors and registration; it does not include all biological or registration uncertainty.

Whole-section validation

Five contiguous blocks of physical sections are reserved in turn. Neighboring guard sections are excluded from training. Anatomy levels, coordinate scaling and basis locations are learned from training cells only; held-out expression does not determine them. All test cells remain together with their original section. Final maps fitted to all reference cells are clearly distinguished from these out-of-sample predictions.

Each gene is compared with a training-only anatomy-mean model and simple spatial nearest-neighbor interpolation. The retained metrics include RMSE, MAE, predictive-interval coverage and width, with observed zero and positive-count behavior examined separately. Fixed alternative spatial scales and shrinkage strengths assess dependence on priors. Test results are not used to rename an unsuccessful model as validated.

One donor supports within-donor spatial prediction, not independent animal replication, cross-donor performance or generalization to exposure. Whole-section blocking reduces local information leakage but does not eliminate all spatial dependence or model misspecification.

Interpretation and limits

A finer display grid, smoother field or narrow conditional credible interval does not increase the acquisition resolution. Predictions at existing positions do not create cells in the approximately 200 µm gaps between sampled sections, and this model generates no extra cells or hemisphere. Reliable resolution enhancement would require independent measurements demonstrating recovery of that additional detail.

No cell-type-specific PM2.5 response is fitted here. Baseline spatial priors and regional bulk changes cannot uniquely identify such responses. The original PM2.5 3D scenario retains its uniform regional multiplier. Posterior baseline uncertainty is not an exposure-effect interval.

## Interpretation and limits

A finer display grid, smoother field or narrow conditional credible interval does not increase the acquisition resolution. Predictions at existing positions do not create cells in the approximately 200 µm gaps between sampled sections, and this model generates no extra cells or hemisphere. Reliable resolution enhancement would require independent measurements demonstrating recovery of that additional detail.

**No cell-type-specific PM2.5 response is fitted here.** Baseline spatial priors and regional bulk changes cannot uniquely identify such responses. The original PM2.5 3D scenario retains its uniform regional multiplier. Posterior baseline uncertainty is not an exposure-effect interval.

Functional enrichment: GO Biological Process

Analysis date

19 October 2026

Questions and analytical scope

Two classical gene-set approaches summarize completed gene-level RNA-seq contrasts. Overrepresentation analysis (ORA) asks whether an annotation occurs more often among FDR-selected genes than expected in the mapped testable gene universe. Preranked gene-set enrichment analysis (GSEA) asks whether genes belonging to a set tend to accumulate toward either end of the complete quality-eligible signed Wald-statistic ranking. The approaches use different inputs and different multiplicity procedures, so agreement is informative but is not required. (Ashburner et al. 2000; Subramanian et al. 2005; Fang et al. 2023)

The PM hypothalamus and cortex comparisons are kept separate. The four ozone age-by-genotype contrasts are added only after their corresponding Wald tables are complete. These are six possible contrast entries, not six exchangeable cohorts. No gene lists, animals, p values, or causal claims are pooled across brain regions, exposure agents, or experimental protocols. The unsigned global ozone LRT and miRNA array features are not supplied as signed mRNA rankings.

Interpretation: GO terms overlap and share ancestors. A large number of significant term labels does not represent the same number of independent mechanisms. GSEA’s gene-set permutations do not capture biological-replicate uncertainty or preserve gene–gene correlations. Enrichment is exploratory evidence about ranked or selected gene sets, rather than proof of pathway activation, suppression, or a cell-specific pollutant mechanism.

Materials and methods

Mouse annotation, mapping, and reproducibility

Gene sets are built from the downloaded mouse MGI GAF and GO Basic ontology, with source URLs, release-header dates, checksums, and derived GMT provenance retained. Their individual source dates are preserved; they should not be treated as a newly generated matched annotation release. Only positive mouse Biological Process annotations are used. NOT annotations are excluded, while positive annotation evidence codes, including electronic evidence, are retained.

Term memberships propagate through active is_a and part_of Biological Process ancestors. Regulatory edges and cross-aspect relations are not used for propagation. Alternative IDs and obsolete terms resolve only when an unambiguous active replacement is available. (Ashburner et al. 2000)

Mapping uses case-sensitive canonical mouse symbols or uniquely resolving GAF synonyms. It does not uppercase genes, infer human orthologs, or match free-text gene descriptions. When several DE features map to one symbol, the representative is selected by the highest baseMean, with lexical feature-ID tie-breaking, independently of effect direction and significance. Ambiguous and unmapped symbols remain visible in the mapping audit.

ORA background, foreground, and FDR

The ORA universe comprises unique mapped representative genes with a finite adjusted DE p value. Mapped genes without a specific GO-BP annotation remain in the universe. Higher- and lower-expression foregrounds use gene-level DE FDR < 0.05 and the sign of the original unshrunken coefficient, without an additional effect-size threshold.

Eligible GO terms have 15–500 members after intersection with the contrast-specific universe. A one-sided hypergeometric survival probability tests the observed or greater overlap. Terms with zero overlap remain in the tested family with p = 1. BH correction is applied across the entire eligible term family, separately for each contrast and each direction. ORA’s padj_BH is therefore distinct from a gene-level DE adjusted p value. (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995)

Preranked GSEA and gene-test quality

The ranking includes unique mapped representatives with both a finite unshrunken Wald statistic and a finite unshrunken-test p value after the source analysis’s validity checks. A finite statistic alone is insufficient: Cook-filtered or otherwise unreliable tests can retain a numerical statistic while having an unavailable p value. These rows are excluded from GSEA, and the exclusions are exported. For ozone, the source analysis also withholds tests for nonconverged fits and genes with zero counts in all six animals of the corresponding stratum. The pre-filter audit p values are not substituted for those unavailable valid-test p values. This is a gene-test quality decision, not an exclusion of an animal.

Genes that have a finite unshrunken-test p value but a missing adjusted p value because of independent filtering remain eligible for ranking. This applies to the PM analyses where that filtering was enabled; ozone uses a fixed count prefilter without adaptive independent filtering. No gene-level FDR or fold-change threshold selects the ranked genes. Genes are ordered by descending signed statistic, with gene-symbol tie-breaking. Term membership is additionally restricted to 15–500 genes in the resulting rank. (Subramanian et al. 2005; Fang et al. 2023)

GSEApy uses 1,000 gene-set/gene-label permutations, weight 1, and seed 20261018. These are not phenotype-label permutations. The reported fdr_qvalue is the GSEA algorithmic normalized-enrichment-score FDR estimate, not BH correction. A nominal permutation p value or estimated q value of zero is a finite-permutation result and does not mean zero error probability.

Why mapping and test families matter

The DE table, mapped gene representatives, ORA universe, and GSEA rank can have different sizes. Mapping losses and duplicate-symbol choices account for some differences. Where used in the PM analyses, independent filtering changes ORA eligibility, while GSEA retains quality-eligible finite-p genes even when their adjusted p value is unavailable. Source DE validity-exclusion counts can also differ from mapped GSEA exclusions because a source feature may be unmapped or removed by the declared duplicate-symbol rule. Ozone ORA foregrounds use the corresponding within-stratum gene-level BH values, rather than the supplementary across-four-strata column.

Biological interpretation

Positive NES denotes association toward the higher-expression end of the specific exposure contrast; negative NES denotes association toward the lower-expression end. Neither sign establishes biochemical pathway activity or the cell population responsible. The same genes can contribute to many related GO terms, and annotation coverage is unequal across genes. Classical ORA does not explicitly model gene-length or detectability biases; the testable background limits, but does not remove, those concerns.

The molecular models and sample sizes remain those of the original regional experiments. An apparent shared term across cortex, hypothalamus, or an ozone stratum is a hypothesis for targeted comparison, not a pooled causal estimate. Cell composition, sampling, age, genotype, exposure delivery, and technical differences can contribute to apparent convergence. No validated target map is supplied for the miRNA array, so mRNA GO analysis is not inferred from miRNA identifiers.

### Why mapping and test families matter

The DE table, mapped gene representatives, ORA universe, and GSEA rank can have different sizes. Mapping losses and duplicate-symbol choices account for some differences. Where used in the PM analyses, independent filtering changes ORA eligibility, while GSEA retains quality-eligible finite-p genes even when their adjusted p value is unavailable. Source DE validity-exclusion counts can also differ from mapped GSEA exclusions because a source feature may be unmapped or removed by the declared duplicate-symbol rule. Ozone ORA foregrounds use the corresponding **within-stratum** gene-level BH values, rather than the supplementary across-four-strata column.

## Biological interpretation

Positive NES denotes association toward the higher-expression end of the specific exposure contrast; negative NES denotes association toward the lower-expression end. Neither sign establishes biochemical pathway activity or the cell population responsible. The same genes can contribute to many related GO terms, and annotation coverage is unequal across genes. Classical ORA does not explicitly model gene-length or detectability biases; the testable background limits, but does not remove, those concerns.

The molecular models and sample sizes remain those of the original regional experiments. An apparent shared term across cortex, hypothalamus, or an ozone stratum is a hypothesis for targeted comparison, not a pooled causal estimate. Cell composition, sampling, age, genotype, exposure delivery, and technical differences can contribute to apparent convergence. No validated target map is supplied for the miRNA array, so mRNA GO analysis is not inferred from miRNA identifiers.

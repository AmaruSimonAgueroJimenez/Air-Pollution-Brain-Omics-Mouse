# Reproduction of the two PM2.5 brain studies

The purpose of this audit is to distinguish recovering a published calculation from demonstrating a biological effect in new animals. We examined the original articles, available supplementary methods, GEO count deposits and the cortex authors' Zenodo code and result tables. Existing primary analyses in this repository were preserved.

For the cortex study ([Zhang et al., Science, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adu4132); [GSE297423](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE297423)), the deposited [author code and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15374632) establish a DESeq2 model with all 20 animals, five conditions and four animals per condition. The author and GEO count matrices are identical. We independently reimplemented the reviewed steps, retaining 21,388 genes with at least ten counts in at least four animals, fitting `~group`, using the default `results()` independent-filtering alpha of 0.1 and then BH FDR <0.05 for discoveries. The alpha used to optimize independent filtering and the final discovery threshold are distinct. Shrinkage used apeglm. Original code was inspected rather than executed wholesale.

The independent fit recovers all four original FDR discovery sets exactly: 109 genes for PFF, 1,205 for PM2.5, 1,222 for PM–PFF, and 639 for PM–PFF followed by PM2.5, each versus PBS. There are zero discordant significant genes, with Jaccard similarity 1.0 for every contrast. Across all 21,388 retained genes, the largest absolute difference in unshrunken log2 effects is less than 8.12×10⁻⁵; nominal and adjusted P-value missingness also match. The 582 PM-specific genes and the 323 genes exclusive to the PM/PM–PFF pair are reproduced. The latter is an **exclusive intersection**, not the entire PM/PM–PFF overlap.

The author-code PCA also recovers 29.2% and 9.8% variance for PC1 and PC2 at the published rounding. Its filter is the count rule above; the article prose instead describes `filterByExpr`. We document this discrepancy and follow the deposited code for this computational benchmark. The original model uses all 20 animals to estimate the dispersion structure, whereas the repository's separate primary PM/PBS comparison uses eight. Its 1,167 discoveries should therefore not be presented as a failed recovery of the 1,205-gene original result.

The deposited GO results contain 13, 365, 266 and 271 significant terms for PFF, PM, PM–PFF and PM–PFF followed by PM, respectively. These are **verified author-table contents**, not independently recomputed enrichment. The source code uses GO biological processes, 20–500 genes per set and apeglm-shrunken log2 effects for ranking. Exact original annotation/package versions and a random seed were not recovered. The immune leading-edge forest plot is a source-selected descriptive view, with pointwise Wald uncertainty; its displayed-gene selection is not multiplicity adjusted.

A separate diagnostic makes a material limitation visible: Ddx3y, Uty and Kdm5d are among the original highest-ranking Wald genes, and every PBS animal has zero original counts for all three Y-linked markers. Other conditions contain both near-zero and high values. The corresponding apeglm effects shrink toward zero. The all-animal marker table and Xist/Y-marker plots justify checking independent sample-sex and identity metadata and possible technical explanations. Expression alone cannot assign sex or establish confounding. We neither removed animals nor invented a sex covariate. Exact recovery of an original model does not establish its causal interpretation.

For the hypothalamus study ([Rajagopalan et al., JCI, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI137315); [GSE145840](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE145840)), the [official supplement](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/137315/sd/pdf/render/1) describes GENCODE M13 protein-coding genes and RUVg correction using 5,000 empirical control genes. Their identifiers and the number of unwanted factors, `k`, were not recovered. The control-selection wording is internally ambiguous, and the main figure and supplement give different abundance statements. An exact original fit is therefore unavailable from the recovered inputs. Proprietary IPA results cannot be reconstructed from the public counts alone.

We instead provide an explicit unadjusted sensitivity analysis of all 11 hypothalamus animals: four filtered-air controls, three PM animals and four high-fat-diet animals in separate groups. The four HFD files have 91 extra features; we used the common 50,417 gene identifiers, excluding the extra features without zero imputation. Of these, 21,932 match M13 protein-coding symbols; the declared CPM >2 in at least three animals rule retains 13,398 genes. TMM/voom, limma moderated tests and BH adjustment were followed by |log2FC| >0.8 and AveExpr >1 gates. This reconstruction yields zero PM and 3,600 HFD genes. The published supplement diagram shows 17 PM and 761 HFD genes. These differences are method sensitivity, **not evidence of biological nonreplication**, because the original RUVg factors, control IDs and exact gene universe are unavailable. The existing primary seven-animal PM/air analysis remains separate.

The new figures supply a numerical reproduction benchmark, multi-condition effect heatmap and intersection matrix, source-selected effect intervals, all-animal marker QC, and a hypothalamus method-sensitivity analysis. Every plot has downloadable source tables; every figure is exported as PNG, SVG and PDF. These are bulk tissue data. They do not add exposed spatial or single-cell measurements, and they do not validate the separate 3D atlas perturbation as an observed spatial response.
